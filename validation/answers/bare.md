## Three-week suggestion

**Facts from the scenario:** one detailed repair video drew views but few bookings; one contrarian post drew arguments but almost no relevant bookings; capacity is eight appointments a week. **Inference:** neither reach nor debate has yet shown that it attracts suitable customers. I would not copy a large account or commit to daily posting on this evidence.

- **Week 1:** Publish a repair case showing the fault, diagnosis, before/after, likely service fit, and a clear booking step. Keep the offer and booking path fixed. Track where inquiries came from.
- **Week 2:** Test a few distinct, service-relevant angles—such as common repair symptoms, a short diagnostic demonstration, and a restoration outcome. Keep the booking step comparable so the topics can be compared.
- **Week 3:** Repeat the best angle with a new example and make a modest follow-up change, such as a clearer service-area or price-range explanation. Release no more than eight appointments; pause booking promotion when they are filled.

Separate **views** (exposure) from **qualified demand** (inquiries that fit the workshop's services and can realistically book). Track views and watch-through as diagnostics, then profile/booking clicks, qualified inquiries, inquiry-to-booking rate, attended appointments, and capacity used. The practical success measure is suitable, completed bookings, not views or arguments. Risks: three weeks may be too small and noisy to identify a winner; platform reach may vary; a topic can attract the wrong audience; and demand above capacity can harm service quality. These are risks, not observed outcomes.
